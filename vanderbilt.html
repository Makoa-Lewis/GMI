Why a chapter

Music City has students who never get the chance.

Nashville is known around the world for music. Yet just outside the city, many students in rural, low-income communities have no access to private lessons, no way to get to them, and no instrument to practice on.

GMI began through Immersion Vanderbilt with a simple idea: the barriers keeping young people from music are practical ones, and practical problems can be solved. The Vanderbilt chapter is where we prove it, starting close to home.

Talent is everywhere. Access isn't. The chapter exists to close that gap, one student at a time.

It's also the engine behind GMI's bigger work: the chapter organizes the Sarangi Documentation Project in Nepal and is developing the model that will let students on other campuses start chapters of their own, the beginnings of a growing global network.

Our goals

What the founding chapter is working toward.

Goal 1 · Teach

Private lessons for rural students

Provide private music instruction to students in low-income rural communities around Nashville, starting small and growing to 15 to 20 students.

Goal 2 · Remove barriers

Cover rides and instruments

Use federal and university grants and humanitarian scholarships so transportation and instruments are never the reason a student can't take part.

Goal 3 · Partner

Work with Tennessee's music community

Collaborate with local music schools, arts programs, and musicians so students get excellent teaching and lasting support.

Goal 4 · Go global

Lead the Nepal project

Organize the Sarangi Documentation Project and its 2027 student cohort, documenting a living tradition in Nepal.

Goal 5 · Grow

Grow the network

Turn what we learn into a clear, repeatable model so students at other universities can launch GMI chapters of their own, each connected to partner communities in Nepal and around the world. See the five-year plan

How it works

From a student's first lesson onward.

  1. 1

    Connect

    We work with schools and community partners to find students who want to learn but lack access.

  2. 2

    Equip

    Each student gets an instrument and a reliable way to get to lessons, covered by grants and scholarships.

  3. 3

    Teach

    Students take regular private lessons with skilled teachers, building real musical skill over time.

  4. 4

    Grow

    Students perform, connect with the wider music community, and see where music can take them.
