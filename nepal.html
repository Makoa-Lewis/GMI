The Sarangi Documentation Project

We're creating the first thorough documentary and written record of an oral storytelling tradition carried on the sarangi by Gandharva musicians in rural, high-altitude Nepal.

WhereRural, high-altitude Nepal
FieldworkMay – mid-June 2027
PartnerThe Initiatives Foundation
MakingDocumentary + archive
The tradition

Stories carried on four strings.

The sarangi is a small, bowed string instrument carved from a single piece of wood. In Nepal, it's closely tied to the Gandharva, a community of hereditary musicians.

For generations, Gandharva musicians have used the sarangi to carry songs and stories from village to village: news, history, epics, and everyday life, set to music. The tradition is passed down by ear, from one generation to the next.

Much of this repertoire lives only in memory and performance. It has never been thoroughly recorded.

This project documents that tradition carefully and respectfully, working with our partners in Nepal, so it can be heard, studied, and passed on.

What we're making

A film, and a record that lasts.

Short documentary

A film that brings the music, the musicians, and their stories to audiences around the world.

Audio and video archive

Full recordings of performances and interviews, preserved so they aren't lost.

Written record

A written account of the songs, stories, and practice behind them, for researchers and future musicians.

Our partner in Nepal

The Initiatives Foundation

The Initiatives Foundation is our partner on the ground in Nepal, led by its director, Karun Dhakal.

The foundation helps shape the project from the start: planning the fieldwork with us, connecting us with Gandharva musicians and their communities, and making sure the work is done carefully and respectfully, on the community's terms.

This project is built together. The Initiatives Foundation brings local knowledge and relationships; GMI brings the recording, filmmaking, and archive work to preserve the tradition and share it with the world.
